About Twitch → Kick Bridge

Version

Shows which of the streamers you follow on Twitch also have a Kick channel, whether they're live there, and a one-click link to their Kick profile. It runs entirely in your browser: there is no server of ours in between.

What refresh (↻) does

Press ↻ in the popup (or “Check now” when the list is empty). It works in two steps:

  1. It counts your follows. One small request asks Twitch how many streamers you follow right now and compares that with the number saved with your list.
  2. Then it decides.
    • Same number: your list is kept and the normal refresh runs on your current feed. It updates live status for everyone known to be on Kick (50 per request) and checks anyone who was never checked, failed before, was saved by an older version, or was marked “not on Kick” more than a week ago.
    • Number went up or down: it fetches your full follow list, saves it with the new number, and rebuilds the feed. Streamers you unfollowed disappear, new follows appear and get checked. The status line says what changed, for example “Follows 951 → 954: 3 new follows found, checking them now.”

Shift+click always fetches the full list and re-checks everyone. Use it after following one streamer and unfollowing another, which leaves the count unchanged.

The icon spins while it works. Hover it to see how long ago everything was updated, or the progress of a running check. If your Twitch login has expired you'll be asked to connect again.

What runs automatically
  • The first time (nothing saved yet): it loads your follows and checks them all in the background.
  • Followed streamers: refreshed every hour by default (you can choose 5, 10 or 30 minutes, or off, in Settings → General → Automatic refresh) while Chrome is running. It does what ↻ does: looks for new follows, refreshes live status for everyone on Kick, and checks anyone not checked yet. New follows are picked up this way only while your Twitch login is valid; otherwise press ↻ in the popup.
  • Favorites: checked every hour by default (or every 5, 10 or 30 minutes), and right after you star someone. One request covers all of them.
  • An interrupted check (Chrome restarted, the background worker was stopped) carries on by itself.
  • Opening the popup shows what's saved instantly and never starts a check. If the saved follow list is over an hour old it quietly refreshes the list from Twitch (a Twitch-only request).

Nothing else runs on its own. Set followed streamers to “Off” and live status changes only when you press ↻.

How streamers are matched, and the warning labels

Kick channels are matched by name (underscores become hyphens), so the Kick account with the same name isn't always the same person. Each match gets a level:

  • Confirmed: the streamer's own Twitch bio links to the Kick channel (only they can write that), or the Kick channel is verified and has the same profile picture.
  • Likely: Kick-verified, or the same profile picture.
  • Unconfirmed: same name and nothing else to go on. Labeled “unconfirmed (same username)”.
  • Possible impersonator: a different profile picture, not Kick-verified, and a tiny Kick following (under 100 followers, or under 500 if they're a Twitch partner). It shows a ⚠ warning, sits below your other matches, never counts as live, can't be starred, and is left out of the toolbar badge and notifications.

A link in the Kick bio back to Twitch is not counted as proof, since anyone can write that. The verified badge, follower count and picture come from a Kick page that isn't an official API and can fail; when it does, the match can't rise above “unconfirmed”. Pictures are compared by a small fingerprint, so a real streamer with a different avatar and a tiny Kick audience can be flagged. Use “It's them” to correct it.

Checking speed, batches and rate limits

Checks are spread evenly. For example, 200 per 5 minutes is one check every 1.5 seconds. A long list is worked through batch after batch until every streamer is checked, even if you close the popup or restart Chrome. Settings → General shows how long your list will take, with a live countdown to the next batch.

If Kick asks us to slow down, the check waits (1, 2, 4, 8, then 10 minutes between tries) and carries on with the same streamer. It gives up only after 12 rate limits in a row, about an hour and a half, and leaves the rest for you to restart with ↻. A streamer that can't be checked is skipped (see Diagnostics) so one failure doesn't stop the rest. A change to the speed setting reaches a running check within ten streamers.

Favorites and notifications

Star up to 10 streamers (only those on Kick). They stay at the top of the list and their live status is checked every hour by default, or as often as you choose. With notifications on (Settings → Favorites), you're told when a favorite goes live: one check saw them offline and the next sees them live. The first check after starring only notes their state, so starring someone who is already live stays quiet. Clicking a notification opens their Kick channel.

A stream that starts and ends between two checks can't be seen (a shorter interval helps), and Chrome has to be running. Possible impersonators are never tracked. The toolbar badge (Settings → General) shows how many followed streamers were live on Kick at the last check.

Using the popup
  • Search filters by name. While you search, streamers who aren't on Kick are shown dimmed so you can see they aren't. “Live only” shows just who's live, and “show not on Kick” shows everyone.
  • The sun/moon button next to Settings switches between light and dark mode. By default the extension follows your computer's setting; Settings → General → Display lets you turn that off.
  • Hover a status line to see why a match got its level.
  • Kick ↗ opens the channel in a background tab so the popup stays open.
  • Not them / It's them correct a match. “It's them” shows an Undo for 5 minutes; Settings → Matches keeps an Undo for good.
Your corrections and backups

Your corrections always win over the automatic result. Settings → Backup saves your corrections, favorites and settings to a file you can restore later. Backups never include API keys, secrets, your Twitch or Kick logins, or cached lookups. “Clear cache” removes saved lookups, the follow list, saved pictures, cached tokens and error history, and stops a running check. Your logins, API keys, settings, favorites and corrections stay.

Where your data lives

Everything is stored in this browser's extension storage: your API keys, login tokens, saved lookups, favorites, corrections and settings, plus small thumbnails of the profile pictures so they load quickly. Nothing is sent to us, because there is no server. The extension talks only to:

  • Twitch: api.twitch.tv and id.twitch.tv, plus profile pictures from static-cdn.jtvnw.net, and the twitch.tv pages it adds its button to.
  • Kick: api.kick.com and id.kick.com, kick.com for profile details, and files.kick.com for pictures.

The Twitch login only asks to read who you follow. The Kick login only asks to read your profile and isn't used for anything yet. Removing the extension deletes all of this, so export a backup first.

Known limits
  • Kick has no public follow action, so “Kick ↗” opens their profile and you follow there.
  • Matching is by name. Read the labels, and correct the ones we get wrong.
  • Automatic checks miss streams that start and end between two checks. A plain ↻ can't see a follow plus an unfollow that cancel out (Shift+click can).
  • The Kick profile page isn't an official API and may change or be blocked.
  • Kick's rate limits aren't published. The default pace is a safe guess that you can lower.
Troubleshooting
  • Diagnostics shows token status, recent errors, rate-limit state and cache size. “Copy diagnostics” never includes keys or tokens.
  • API keys has “Test a lookup” to check your Kick and Twitch setup.
  • Nothing showing up? Check Connections and API keys, then press ↻ in the popup.
  • Something looks stale or stuck? Clear cache in Diagnostics, then open the popup to start fresh.
